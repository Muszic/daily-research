# A Preliminary Search for Evidence on Government Software Engineering Practices: Results from Three Rapid Reviews

- **Category:** Software Engineering
- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22485v1

---
This paper presents a preliminary investigation into the availability of academic evidence on government software engineering (SE) practices.

### Problem

Government agencies are significant drivers of digital transformation and major developers of software, yet there is a striking **lack of empirical evidence** on their specific Software Engineering (SE) practices within academic literature. This scarcity hinders the adoption of Evidence-Based Software Engineering (EBSE) in the public sector, as practitioners lack scientifically validated insights from contexts similar to their own. Previous research and practitioner perceptions (e.g., from Uruguay's e-government agency, AGESIC) have highlighted this gap, creating a barrier to informed decision-making in government SE.

### Method

To assess the current state of evidence, the researchers conducted **three complementary rapid reviews (RRs)**, focusing on publications from 2024 as a recent snapshot. The rapid review methodology was chosen for its efficiency and resource constraints. The search strategy involved manually screening publications from:

1.  **RR1: Top-tier international SE venues** (e.g., Information and Software Technology (IST) Journal, ESEM, Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE) Journal, ICSE).
2.  **RR2: Regional SE conferences** in South America (e.g., CLEI, CIbSE, SBES).
3.  **RR3: A curated newsletter** specifically focused on public-sector digitalization (EDOS).

Study selection and data extraction were performed by junior researchers under senior supervision, with initial classification for RR3 assisted by ChatGPT and subsequently validated. The research question guiding this work was: "What evidence exists on governments’ software-engineering practices?"

### Impact

Out of a total of **984 screened papers** across all three rapid reviews, the study identified **only four publications** that reported on SE practices conducted by, for, or in collaboration with government bodies.

Key findings and implications include:

*   **Low Visibility:** The results strongly confirm the perception that academic evidence on government SE practices has very low visibility in mainstream SE venues (RR1 yielded zero relevant papers).
*   **Characteristics of Existing Evidence:** The few identified papers were mostly short **experience reports or case studies**, predominantly published in **regional venues**, and some in **non-English languages**. Informally, they were also noted as not qualifying as high-quality studies.
*   **Significant Gap:** This limited output indicates a significant gap in the available, accessible, and high-quality empirical evidence for supporting EBSE in the public sector.
*   **Future Directions:** The authors suggest several implications for addressing this problem:
    *   The need for **dedicated publication venues** or tracks focused on government SE.
    *   **Incentives for collaboration** between academia and government agencies to encourage research and publication.
    *   Improved methods for identifying and evaluating **grey literature** (e.g., internal government reports, policy documents), which likely contains valuable, but currently inaccessible, information on government SE practices.
*   This preliminary study provides a crucial understanding of the current evidence landscape and outlines directions for future, more extensive research to better support evidence-based software engineering in the public sector.
